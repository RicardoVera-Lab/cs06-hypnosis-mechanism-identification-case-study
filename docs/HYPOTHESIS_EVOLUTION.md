# Hypothesis Evolution

## Phase 1 — Strong intuitive story

**Starting idea:** surprise or interruption may open a special window in which suggestion becomes unusually effective.

## Phase 2 — Decomposition

The investigation separated the intuitive story into multiple rival explanations rather than allowing one narrative to explain every response.

## Phase 3 — Discrimination

The surviving explanations were tested for whether they could be distinguished in principle with plausible observables.

A key result emerged: measurement error itself can create a misleading appearance of a separate mechanism.

## Phase 4 — External evidence audit

Existing secondary data were evaluated against the minimum observable structure required by the computational models.

No dataset met the full decisive standard.

## Phase 5 — Calibration

Real-world source information was used only where it legitimately constrained noise, reliability or covariance.

Sources were not pooled into a fictional joint sample.

## Phase 6 — Closure

The laboratory closed the case with bounded unresolved questions.

That closure is a result: the next meaningful evidence would require a new primary-data research program.
